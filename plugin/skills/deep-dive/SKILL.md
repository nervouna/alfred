# Deep dive

1. **Frame it.** State the question, the scope (time span, region, what is out of scope) and what the user will use the answer for. When the scope is unclear in a way that changes the result, ask one question first.
2. **Primary sources first**: official announcements, filings, papers, laws and regulations, original datasets, first-hand statements. Use news and analysis for context and to find the primary sources, and cite the primary one when you have it.
3. **Build a timeline** of dated events (YYYY-MM-DD, or as precise as the source allows), each with its source.
4. **Cross-check the key facts.** Numbers, dates and who said what need at least two independent sources. When sources disagree, give both, say which is more credible and why.
5. **Keep three things apart**: established facts, claims someone reported, and your own inference. Label inference as such.
6. **Broad topics**: split the work into independent threads and delegate as the delegation rules say, one researcher per thread, then merge and check their findings.
7. **Structure the result:**
   1. 结论: three to five bullets.
   2. 背景.
   3. 时间线.
   4. 分析.
   5. 未能确认的事项: what could not be confirmed, and what would settle it.
   6. 来源.

   A long result goes into a file where the workspace rules put deliverables (with charts, use the html-report skill), and the reply carries the conclusions.
